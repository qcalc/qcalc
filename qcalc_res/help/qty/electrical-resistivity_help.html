Electrical resistivity is a measure of how strongly a material opposes the flow of electric current through it. It is a property that determines how well a material can conduct electricity, with higher resistivity indicating a greater resistance to the flow of electrons.

Common units used to measure electrical resistivity include ohm-meter (Ω·m), ohm-centimeter (Ω·cm), and ohm-inch (Ω·in). These units are used to quantify the resistance of various materials to the flow of electricity, allowing for the comparison of different materials based on their conductive properties.

  • Electrical resistivity is crucial in determining the efficiency of electrical conductors.
  • It plays a key role in the design and performance of electronic devices.
  • Understanding resistivity helps in predicting and preventing electrical failures in various systems.
