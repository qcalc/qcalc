Specific energy is a physical quantity that represents the amount of energy per unit mass of a substance. It is a measure of the energy content of a material on a per unit mass basis.

Radioactive dose is a measure of the amount of ionizing radiation absorbed by a material or living tissue. It is used to quantify the potential harmful effects of radiation exposure on humans and the environment.

Some common examples of related units of measurements include:

  • Gray (Gy): the SI unit of absorbed radiation dose
  • Sievert (Sv): the SI unit of equivalent dose, which takes into account the biological effect of different types of radiation
  • Becquerel (Bq): the SI unit of radioactivity, representing the number of radioactive decays per second

Application and Importance: - Used in radiation therapy to treat cancer - Helps in assessing radiation exposure risks for workers in nuclear facilities - Important in monitoring environmental radiation levels for public safety
